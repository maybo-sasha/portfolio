AI builds the journey. Marketers stay in control.

Streams turns a marketing brief into a customer journey. Marketers can review the steps, check expected reach and understand the AI decisions behind each send.

The challenge

When AI builds the journey, what does the marketer need to see?

Generating a journey leaves the marketer responsible for a plan they did not assemble. The challenge was making its assumptions, reach and automated choices clear enough to review and change.

I owned the end-to-end UX process, defining the product solutions and making the UX decisions in collaboration with stakeholders from the product and engineering teams.

Discovery & definition

Designing the work around the AI

Team walkthroughs focused the design on three questions: how to express intent, where to review the journey and how much control to give each agent.

01 · Starting point

Let the brief set the pace

I designed one composer for a complete plan, an idea built line by line or a recipe. Marketers can start with what they know without mapping every step first.

02 · Workspace

Keep the journey in context

I kept the canvas beside chat so an answer has a visible place in the journey. The workspace adapts to show the relevant audience, results or decisions.

03 · Control

Choose how much the AI decides

I gave every Stream one autonomy control with three levels. Fixed runs it exactly as built. Assisted suggests changes only when asked. Supervised stages each content and timing choice for sign-off. Every Stream opens at Supervised, so nothing reaches a customer before the marketer approves it.

UX decisions & flows

Make every handoff clear

I separated build approval from launch. Approval moves the journey into a dry run, where findings can lead back to the canvas for a fix. Starting the Stream remains an explicit decision.

Suggestions are optional, so reviewing advice does not become a required detour.

Three screens, three responsibilities

Each screen has one job to make clear, marked on its wireframe. Beneath it, the same screen in three passes on one layout: a sketch for placement, a wireframe for the grid and hierarchy, then the design system applied.

01 · Lobby

Prioritize active work

The composer leads, and running Streams sit under the program summary, ahead of drafts.

02 · Canvas

Keep the journey central

The journey anchors the workspace, with the AI panel on the left and the outcome card on the right.

03 · Build review

Compare intent with the build

The brief sits beside the generated steps, and suggestions arrive one at a time under it.

From brief to build

Make the generated plan reviewable

I combined free-text input with audience and flow-block shortcuts, so marketers can describe an idea and reference existing building blocks in the same brief.

The journey appears step by step, and the brief comes back as a card to approve. The walkthrough builds an at-risk customer journey from one brief.

Review before launch

Advice with evidence, a check with fixes

Suggestions show their tradeoff. The send-time card compares one fixed hour with each customer's best hour inside the allowed window, so the benefit is concrete before anyone accepts it.

Accepting is reversible. The card turns into an Added state that names the three campaigns it changed, with Undo beside it.

Every dry-run finding comes with a fix. Here 29K customers would never get the last SMS, so the finding offers an email for them. One click adds it and the canvas shows the changed step. Forecasts stay labelled as modelled estimates.

The lobby

Move from program health to a specific Stream

I placed program performance above running Streams to establish a clear review hierarchy. The summary covers the whole program even when the list is filtered.

Cards expose goals, progress and review needs; breakdowns appear on hover. This keeps the overview compact while making supporting detail available before opening a journey.

The canvas

Keep results beside the journey

I placed suggestions and decision activity on the left, with outcomes on the right. The journey stays between them, connecting an AI choice to the step it affects and the performance of the wider Stream.

Layers

Reveal detail without losing the flow

Showing every audience count, timing rule and AI choice at once would overwhelm the canvas. I organized that detail into eight layers that marketers can combine around the question they are investigating.

The Customer flow layer shows how people move through the branches; the AI decisions layer shows agent choices. Both use the same steps, so switching focus preserves orientation.

Measure

Balance useful detail with readable steps

I limited each step to four visible metrics to keep the canvas readable. Marketers choose from twelve measures, bringing the figures relevant to their goal onto the journey.

Dry-run forecasts remain separate from running-Stream results, keeping modelled estimates distinct from observed performance.

Decisions and insights

Connect each AI choice to its evidence

I connected the activity feed to the affected step, creating a path from a short update to the evidence behind a decision.

Decision activity

Separate advice from actions taken

Recommendations sit above the activity feed and wait for the marketer to apply or dismiss them. The feed below records decisions agents have already made, keeping proposed changes distinct from completed actions.

Decision inspector

Show the alternatives behind a choice

The inspector brings channel, promotion, offer, timing and content decisions into one view. Each record shows the signal, alternatives, allocation and comparison with the marketer's setup, alongside what the system continues to monitor.

Change it by asking

Edit the journey from the conversation

I treated the chat and the canvas as one tool. Anything on the journey can be changed by asking, in the marketer's own words, and nothing moves until they have seen what it changes.

It works at any size, from a timing to a whole new campaign that arrives complete with its AI decisioning already on. All of it happens before the dry run, so the check always runs on the journey that will go live.

Canvas behavior

Build and arrange directly on the canvas

I made the canvas a place to build, not only to view. Drag a step from Flow blocks onto any connector and it slots in between, while the steps below move down to make room. Its template comes from the same Stream, so the step arrives ready.

Grouping, alignment, notes and a day ruler keep a long journey readable. Collapse a group to see the wider flow, expand it to edit single steps. None of it changes the journey logic.
