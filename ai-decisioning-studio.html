Optimization Power AI agents pulling in one direction

A connected workspace for marketers to set a Strategy’s goal, give AI clear boundaries and understand what it contributes across their campaigns.

The brief

One place for every AI decision

What AI decisioning is. Lifecycle marketing has long run on rules: a segment for each audience, a campaign for each segment, and the offer, channel and send time picked by hand, one test at a time. AI decisioning changes who makes those choices. The marketer sets the goal, the creative and the guardrails. AI picks a promotion, a channel and an hour for each customer: on one campaign, one of 78 ways for each of 25,016 customers. It sends at random first to learn, then favors what works.

What we set out to do. Unified AI Decisioning decides the promotion, the channel and the hour together, for every customer, across every campaign in a Strategy. The goal was to show all of that AI decisioning in one holistic place: what it decided, what it changed against a control group, and the people behind the decisions, through customer profiles that turn the data into something a marketer can read and act on.

The challenges

Know your customers. Control your AI. See what it will do.

Product

Every optimization in one place

Setup in one place, reports in another, analytics in a third, and every agent chasing its own metric. Marketers didn’t know where to switch an agent on, or what it would bring.

UX

Thousands of decisions, in human words

Once AI decides per customer, there’s no option list left to inspect. Every decision and result has to read in plain language: what AI did, against what, and for whom.

Trust

Getting large brands to switch AI on

Large e-commerce brands hold back from handing campaigns to AI. They need to see everything AI did, with no black box, and know what it will do and bring before they switch it on.

Discovery & definition

What shaped the page

The page wasn’t designed in one pass. Each input below came with a finding, and each finding changed the page.

  1. July 2026 · Customer walkthrough

    One goal, not one campaign at a time

    A marketer ran 10 to 15 campaigns a month for one objective and opened each one to see if it moved. The Strategy became the starting point: one goal, its customer pool and every campaign that feeds it.

  2. October 2026 · Product review

    A success KPI, and a full page

    Each Strategy gets a success KPI, plus the KPI its promotions optimize for. Campaign work moved from a modal to a full page, with room for setup, results and explanations.

  3. November 2026 · AI Decisioning review

    One KPI, one baseline

    One financial KPI per campaign, and every result read against a control group that got no optimization, so no figure stands without what it is measured against.

  4. November 2026 · Sales demo

    The decision, per person

    Showing what each customer got made the decisions concrete. On the page it became Customer profiles: the groups AI treats alike, in the marketer’s words.

The strategy page

Unified AI Decisioning

Until now each agent answered one question on its own: which offer, which channel, which hour. Unified AI Decisioning answers them together. For every customer it picks one promotion and one channel, and when to send, out of everything the marketer allowed. Each pair it sends is a combination, and a Strategy with eight campaigns runs dozens of them at once.

The design question was how a marketer reads thousands of customer-level decisions without opening every campaign, and trusts them. Once a decision is handed to Optimove there are no option lists left to inspect, so the page had to show where customers actually went, and against what.

Optimove already holds all of it: every decision, its result, and each customer’s attributes. The design work was translation. Each figure says in plain words what it is measured against, and the customers behind the decisions read as people a marketer recognises, not as model output.

The idea

One picture of every decision

A share-of-customers map, promotion by channel, across all campaigns together. The darkest cell is the combination sent most. One glance says what AI leans on, and what it never sends.

The depth

Top combinations, then all of them

The three biggest combinations show their message, content variant, target group, send time and share. Everything else folds into one line with its share, and a link opens the full list.

The reading order

Agents in the order AI decides

Which campaign, then promotion and channel, then when, then which version. Agent Performance follows that order, so the map sits where the marketer meets the decision it explains.

The people

Customer profiles behind the decisions

The groups AI treats alike, as ID cards. Each shows the same attributes, how often the group reached the strategy’s goal against the whole pool, and the promotion, channel and hour that worked for them. Best result first.

From data to people

What Optimove holds, translated

Every decision, its result and each customer’s attributes already exist. The page turns them into a few groups a marketer recognises, each with what worked for it.

The campaign page

Explainability

Five AI agents spend margin and decide what every customer receives. Marketers won’t hand that to a black box, and the largest e-commerce brands are the most hesitant of all. So the campaign page opens with what AI decided and what it changed, and every setup shows what it will do before it is switched on.

The Overview tells the story in one line: which promotion and channel lead, and when most messages go out. Under it, the benefit against a random control group that received no optimization, the customers it reached, and what it decided: the channel split, the combinations map and the send hours. Each agent then has its own tab for the depth, read against the same control group, occurrence by occurrence.

Before and after

What it will do, then what it did

Trust is built on both sides of the switch: a preview of what AI will do before the marketer turns it on, and the result against a random control group once it runs.

Overview

What it decided

One sentence first. Then the optimization benefit beside the customers it was measured on, and three cards: channel, promotion by channel, send time. The result per combination closes the tab.

Promotion and Channel

Learned, then earned

Optimove AI against the random control group, occurrence by occurrence. The first sends go out at random to learn; the line separates once it favors what works. The full result per combination closes the tab.

Send Time

The hour, with evidence

Open and click rate benefit against a control group sent at the brand’s best hours, and the engagement by hour the product already reports, read in place: when customers respond best, and when most messages went out.

Before it runs

What customers will receive, not only what they got

Transparency can’t start after the first send. Setting up Send Time Optimization shows what it will do for every selected action: when its customers respond best, the send window, and how many today’s fixed times reach against sending each customer at their own best hour. The marketer sees the change before switching it on.
