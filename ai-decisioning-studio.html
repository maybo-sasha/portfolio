Optimization Power AI agents pulling in one direction

A connected workspace for marketers to set a program’s goal, give AI clear boundaries and understand what it contributes across their campaigns.

  • Connect a business objective to its customer pool and campaigns
  • Review the options AI can choose before applying a setup

The brief

Give the agents one goal

Optimove’s AI agents could already decide a lot for each customer. Each did it inside its own setup and its own report. The challenge had shifted from switching AI on to directing it: one goal, clear limits and results a marketer can trust.

I worked with stakeholders from the product and engineering teams, from Strategy creation to campaign setup and decision reporting. I owned the product model, the page hierarchy, the interaction flows and the reusable components that connect them.

The challenge

One objective, several campaigns

A marketer ran several campaigns for one business objective, and had to open each of them to see the overall result.

The strategy page

See the whole program

One overview replaces the campaign-by-campaign check. It connects the goal to its customer pool, its coverage and the value AI adds.

I placed audience, reach, benchmarks and results above the campaigns, so marketers see where the program needs work and which campaign to open next.

Audience

Define the customer pool

The pool defines who the Strategy serves. Its size and its join and leave trend give every number on the page the same audience.

Coverage

Keep the gap on the overview

Reached and missed customers sit beside the pool, with the benchmark in view. The gap points to the campaign to improve, or the one to add.

Measurement

Total KPI shows activity. Uplift shows the incremental gain against the control group, with the comparison named beside it. When there is no control, the page says so.

The connected workspace

From a goal to the whole program

From the Strategy lobby into one program and its campaigns. The pool, coverage and impact stay together, with every campaign one step away.

Strategy creation

Describe the audience, then review the criteria

Strategy creation starts with a goal and a customer pool. Marketers reuse a Target Group, or describe a new audience in plain language.

OptiGenie turns the description into rules the marketer can edit, while a summary keeps the audience, control share and KPIs in view at every step.

The campaign page

Make automation inspectable

The campaign page is the decisioning layer marketers can see: setup, results and explanations in one workspace. The header shows the optimization score and what the campaign contributes. Each decisioning card shows its status, its options and the next step.

I kept the campaign details and the inherited offer objective beside the controls, and gave all six agents one pattern for setup and reporting.

Context

Keep campaign context visible

Campaign details and the inherited offer objective stay beside the working area, so every setup change stays tied to the Strategy.

Control

Make the decision boundaries clear

Each agent has its own options and scope. Setup shows what AI can change, and the marketer reviews it before applying.

Explainability

Distinguish choices from reasons

No black box. Every result names its comparison, and recorded decisions show what each customer received, with the inputs and outcomes behind it. A missing reason is stated, not hidden.

OptiGenie

A conversation that moves the work forward

OptiGenie works beside the Strategy or campaign on screen. Marketers ask, and it finds the audience, explains a result or opens the next step, without leaving the page.

Ask, then act

Connect the answer to the interface

“Who wasn’t reached?” returns this Strategy’s missed audience. “Show the pool” brings it into view.

I designed the answer and the page action as one interaction. Visible feedback shows what OptiGenie did, and the workspace stays open to check it.

The agents

Each agent, in action

Six AI agents, each deciding one part of the campaign for every customer. I gave them one way to explain the role, set the limits and inspect the contribution.

Promotion

Picks the promotion each customer receives, from a bank the marketer defines.

The marketer builds a bank of promotions for the campaign: a deposit match, loyalty points, free spins. Optimove AI learns which one works for each customer and sends that one. It only chooses from the bank.

I designed the bank as a plain list, so every promotion AI may use is in view and adding or removing one is a single step. Promotion is decided together with Channel, so the report shows one shared result against the marketer’s original setup.

Channel

Chooses how to reach each customer, from the channels the marketer sets up.

Every channel carries its own template, brand and send window. The marketer sets them up once, and Optimove AI picks the channel that works for each customer.

I kept the template, brand and send window in one channel card. The shared Promotion and Channel report connects those settings to what each customer received.

Journey Decisioning

Picks the right campaign for each customer when campaigns overlap.

When campaigns compete for the same customer, Journey Decisioning predicts which one creates more long-term value. Marketers need to see how that changes the priority they set.

I put fixed and automatic priority side by side in setup. The report connects the campaigns held back to the prediction behind each choice.

Offer Decisioning

Adjusts which of the campaign’s offers each customer group receives.

The Strategy decides whether offers optimize for gross or net revenue. Within that objective, Offer Decisioning learns which offer each customer group responds to.

I kept the inherited objective visible in setup and in reporting. The report shows how customers split across the offers, and the incremental gain against sending one offer to everyone.

Send Time Optimization

Sends at each customer’s best hour, inside the window the marketer allows.

The marketer chooses the hours AI may use. Setup has to show when the audience responds, and how timing works for customers with little history.

I placed the send window over the audience activity chart and made clear which messages it applies to. The report names the fallback hour used when there is too little history.

Content Decisioning

Matches the best-performing content to each customer while the campaign is live.

The marketer defines which variations may be sent. As customers engage, Content Decisioning shifts traffic toward what performs, so the creative has to stay connected to the result.

I separated choosing templates from editing variations. In the report, creative previews, engagement and traffic sit together, so the number has a visible context.
