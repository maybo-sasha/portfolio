Optimove’s first mobile app, built around its AI.

OptiGenie is Optimove’s AI assistant for CRM marketers and the managers who follow their work. I led its first mobile app from the UX and feature set to the MVP, and we are now adding more of what OptiGenie does on desktop. Screens show sample data.

The challenge

Bringing OptiGenie to mobile

OptiGenie is the first mobile app Optimove has built, and a strategic product for the company. I led it from the first UX decisions and feature set to the MVP.

OptiGenie 3.1 had grown on desktop, with memory, artifacts, dashboards and scheduled tasks.

On the phone it serves two users with overlapping needs. CRM marketers want a fast, clear update and a bird’s-eye view of their brands and campaigns, then to act on what needs them. Managers want the picture of the marketing activity without running it. Rather than two apps, both start from the same summary and go as deep as they need: Dashboards for the view at a glance, Explore and Tasks for acting on it.

The work was to turn that into one product for the phone, on the Optimove design system, with the AI’s work visible and in the marketer’s hands.

Discovery & definition

Main UX decisions

Bringing OptiGenie 3.1 from desktop to the phone meant more than fitting it on a smaller screen. On desktop it is a long conversation in a wide window; on a phone, a visit is often a quick check. Every call came back to one question: what does the marketer need in the first few seconds, and what can wait a tap away. These are the decisions that shaped the app most: where you start, where things live, and what the AI does on its own.

01 · Starting point

Open on what matters

The home says what OptiGenie can do and what is coming up next, instead of an empty box waiting for the right question.

02 · Structure

Explore and Dashboards as tabs

Marketers ask new questions, and they check the same numbers every morning. The views they follow get their own tab, refreshed on a schedule, a swipe from where they ask: the conversation is the way in, not the whole product. Everything else sits in one menu, on every screen.

03 · Trust

Visible steps, approval by choice

OptiGenie shows the steps it takes while it works. Whether a task asks before it changes anything in Optimove is the marketer’s call: Ask me first or Run on its own. Sending to customers always asks.

UX decisions & flows

Navigation model

Every screen is a step from Explore or Dashboards, and the menu opens from all of them. Back always goes up one level: a task returns to the task list, a dashboard opened from the full list returns there.

Swipe and tap do the same thing, with the same motion, so the two never feel like different actions.

Motion

Motion and gestures

Motion does two jobs here. It orients: where the finger lands, which way each screen travels, and how every transition tells you where you came from. And it gives the product its personality, carried by the system rather than added screen by screen: the same curves, timings and logo build everywhere.

Motion follows Material 3. Things arrive on a decelerating curve and leave on an accelerating one, and content arrives together rather than item by item. Animations fall back to a still state when reduced motion is on.

01

Thumb zone

The composer and every main action, from New task to Create and Send, sit low on the screen, within reach of one hand.

02

Tab swipe

On the start screens a sideways swipe switches tabs, and the tab thumb glides to the one you land on.

03

Back gesture

Drag a screen to the right and it follows, then leaves on an accelerating curve as the one underneath settles in. The very edge stays the phone’s own gesture.

04

Navigation drawer

It opens on the side its icon sits, decelerating in, and closes with a swipe the other way, the page drifting aside with it.

05

Bottom sheets

Their main action sits at the bottom edge, named for what it does, and a drag down closes them.

06

Toggle and reorder

Pausing a task moves it to Paused and done. The switch slides first, then the card glides to its new group while the others close the gap, so you can follow where it went.

Logo animation

Optimove’s logo is a block and a triangle. The concept was to build it from simple shapes that attach to each other: two circles join into one, the circle becomes a square that shows a prompt, the square pulls apart like gum, a star grows out of the stretch, and when the gum snaps the halves settle into the block and triangle.

Each stage has its own duration and easing, from a slow start through a quick middle to a soft landing, so the build reads as one continuous move of about four seconds. The same animation does two jobs: it opens the app, and it shows that OptiGenie is working.

On launch

Splash state

On the splash the logo builds once and holds while sign-in rises in beneath it, so opening the app and signing in read as one moment.

While it works

Thinking state

While OptiGenie works, the same build loops beside the steps it is taking: each one waits, then gets its check. The loop always lands on the full logo before it starts again.

First-time user experience

Onboarding and first-visit tips

OptiGenie signs in with the marketer’s Optimove account, so their tenants, brands and data are there from the first minute. What a first-time user needs is orientation, not setup: three short screens pick the tenant to start on, show how to ask in your own words, and ask about notifications in plain terms.

After that there is no tour. Each part of the app explains itself the first time it opens, one tip at a time: how Explore and Dashboards relate, how to make a first dashboard, the steps behind an answer, where tasks and artifacts live, and how to switch tenant or theme.

Explore

Answer layout

The home opens on what is coming up and questions worth asking. Each answer leads with its point and key figure, then the chart that fits the data, with the steps OptiGenie took one tap away.

From an answer

Save as dashboard

Any answer can become a dashboard. Pick how often it refreshes, and it joins the views you follow.

Dashboards

Dashboard creation

Creating a dashboard shows OptiGenie working through it: what it reads, how it groups the data, which charts it picks and when it refreshes. Then it joins the tab, which opens on the views you follow, the next one peeking at the edge; a swipe moves between them and a tap opens one live.

Tasks

Scheduled tasks

A task is something OptiGenie does on a schedule, like a morning summary of deposits or a weekly churn check. Three steps: setting it up, following its runs, and approving where it asks.

Artifacts

Artifact management

Live views, reports and templates land in Artifacts, each previewed with its own chart. From the list or inside one: share with people at Optimove or anyone with the link, pause live updates to keep a snapshot, rename, archive or delete.

Dark mode

Dark theme

OptiGenie lives on a phone, read between meetings and late in the day, often in dim light. A dark theme cuts glare there and saves battery on OLED screens, and it lets the figures and charts carry the screen. So the app offers both, and each user picks light or dark in Settings; the choice is remembered on the device. The design system had no dark-mode palette available, so I built one on its colour roles, following mobile best practice:

01 · Not an inversion

Role mapping

Canvas, cards, raised surfaces, text and accent each got their own dark value from the same colour scales, with dark surfaces tinted from the deepest brand purple so they stay on brand.

02 · Elevation

Elevation

On dark, shadows barely show. Depth comes from lighter steps: canvas, then card, then raised sheets and menus, as Apple and Material recommend.

03 · Lighter accents

Accent and chart colours

Links, highlights and chart marks move to lighter shades of the brand purple on dark, so they hold contrast without vibrating. Charts get their own dark steps.

04 · Measured contrast

Contrast

Text at 4.5:1 and controls at 3:1, against WCAG. White text on the design system’s default button purple measured 4.45:1 and failed, so primary buttons moved one shade darker, still with white text, at 6.05:1, in both themes. Higher-contrast settings are respected.

Side by side

Theme comparison

The same question in Explore, “Summarize my dashboard”, in both themes. Surfaces, chart steps and accent each take their own value, so the answer reads as clearly in the dark.

Switching

Theme switch transition

Changing the theme is a circular reveal from where you tap, with a plain crossfade where that isn’t supported.

Where it stands

MVP in beta with selected clients

OptiGenie mobile is in beta. The MVP is with a selected group of clients and is not yet released to the wider public. So far it is a success.

The feedback has been great. Clients love using it for a quick view of their results on the go: the answers are easy to read, and creating a dashboard or a task is quick and simple. That was the aim from the start: complex CRM data turned into answers a marketer can read at a glance and act on.

I work closely with these clients, gathering their feedback and seeing how they use the app day to day. What we learn shapes the next releases, as more of what OptiGenie does on desktop comes to the phone.
